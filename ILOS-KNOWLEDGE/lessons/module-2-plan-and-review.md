# Module 2: Plan and Review

Plan and Review are two pillars that dance together in an eternal cycle. Review honors the past (Water -- feeling, flowing back to source). Planning directs the future (Air -- thinking, reaching toward possibility). Together, they form the heartbeat of intentional living. This is how you build a life, not just a year.

> "Transformation happens in the pauses. When you pause at the beginning of a new cycle, you open space for intention to arise."

---

## The VPER Cycle

The Intentional Life Design (ILD) system is built around a four-phase cycle that repeats at every time scale:

**... --> VISION --> PLAN --> EXECUTE --> REVIEW --> ...**

For each period, the process is:
- **Vision**: Get inspired by an ideal for this period
- **Planning**: Set Clear Goals, projects, and systems for the next period
- **Execute**: Take aligned action toward your goals
- **Review**: Reflect and list all your wins from the completed period

The Review and Plan process flows continuously. Each review completion feeds into a reconnection to vision and a new plan.

---

## Review Before Plan

The system could be called "Review and Plan" -- that is the real order. Before planning a new period, always review the previous one. This frames what you achieved and sets the tone for what comes next.

If you have to choose between planning or reviewing, choose reviewing. Our minds are naturally in planning mode -- we do not need to remind ourselves to plan. What we need is the discipline to stop, breathe, and look backwards before rushing forward.

**Reviews are not report cards. They are celebrations of progress and opportunities for growth.** There is no failure in ILD, only data and progress.

---

## Gap to Gain: The Foundational Mindset

This is the core principle that powers the entire review system:

- **The Gap**: Comparing yourself to an ideal scenario you have not reached. Exhausting. Never enough. Always falling short.
- **The Gain**: Measuring against your past self, where you started. Energizing. Always progressing. Always accumulating evidence of capability.

When you focus on the Gap, you are never enough. When you focus on the Gain, you are always progressing.

**Detachment is the secret to avoiding the Gap.** Strive for big goals, but be flexible. Be unattached to the specific outcome. Always celebrate what you accomplished, even if it was not the original goal. A goal you abandon for something better is not a failure -- it is intelligence.

## Cycles of Time

Life happens in cycles at different scales. Each cycle gets its own Review and Plan rhythm:

| Cycle | Character | Suggested Duration |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Year** | The transformative arc | 3+ hours (can span multiple sessions) |
| **Quarter** | Strategic 13-week cycles | 1-2 hours |
| **Month** | Essential monthly focus | 30-60 minutes |
| **Week** | The core rhythm (highest impact) | 20-30 minutes |
| **Day** | Optional micro-intentions | 5 minutes |

Each cycle is treated with a similar pattern but with adapted depth. The week is the fundamental unit -- the highest-impact habit you can build. Weekly planning without monthly context is just reacting to what is loudest.

## The Review Process

Every review follows three phases:

1. **Celebrate**: See your wins. Feel the progress. Let it land.
2. **Recognize**: Notice patterns. What themes emerge? What worked?
3. **Redirect**: Any adjustments needed for the next period?

**Core Review Questions** (regardless of time scale):
- What did I accomplish? (Wins)
- What did I learn?
- What surprised me?
- How am I different?
- Why am I great?

### Reviews by Time Scale

**Weekly Review**: The foundational practice. Look back at daily wins, assess weekly goals, reflect on what went well and what you would do differently.

**Monthly Review**: Read through all weekly wins. Assess monthly goals. Analyze patterns -- which areas of life got attention, which were neglected? Extract learnings.

**Quarterly Review**: Read through all monthly wins. Assess quarterly goals. Perform strategic analysis -- which High Hard Goals progressed most? Which areas flourished or need attention? Update personal context. Check if vision elements need refinement.

**Annual Review**: The most profound review. 52 weeks of wins. 12 months of progress. 4 quarters of transformation. Reflect on identity evolution -- who were you at year start? Who are you now? Check MTP alignment.

## The Planning Process

### Planning Mindsets

**A new relationship with goals.** Goals in ILD create clarity and direction, not rigid obligations. It is perfectly valid to change a goal mid-period, mark a goal as "Pivoted" when direction shifts, or follow an unexpected opportunity that was not in the plan. The system serves you, not the other way around.

**Planning is ongoing.** A Year Plan is not a blueprint -- it is an ongoing commitment to stop, reflect, and plan the intermediary cycles. You cannot plan once and expect it to hold. The plane always goes off course, then redirects back to the target.

**Hold your goals lightly.** A goal is not a fixed point you must arrive at. It is a compass direction that helps you set sail. The plan is less important than the progress you make. Goals are hypotheses about what might be valuable, not contracts with yourself.

> "Plans are nothing; planning is everything." -- Dwight D. Eisenhower

### Maximum 3 Goals Per Period

Extreme prioritization creates focus:
- 3 yearly goals
- 3 quarterly goals
- 3 monthly goals
- 3 weekly goals
- 1-3 daily intentions (optional)

> "If you have more than 3 priorities you have none." -- Robert Brault

By focusing on few outcomes, you can direct energy and attention to specific goals and make the most progress. You know exactly what matters. You can actually remember them. You can make meaningful progress on each.

### The Planning Sequence

1. **Expand**: List what you want to achieve (brain dump, diverge)
2. **Prioritize**: Choose your top 3 priorities (converge)
3. **Define**: Create clear goal statements
4. **Action**: Identify execution steps

### Goal-Setting Philosophy

**Think Big, Go 10X.** Do not aim for mediocre goals. By choosing something different from what the majority pursues, you face less competition. A bigger future provides enough motivation to make your present better. If you achieve 70% of an ambitious goal, you have made more progress than achieving 100% of a safe goal.

**Optimize for novelty and interestingness.** Novel and interesting pursuits lead to more novel and interesting possibilities. Ask "What would be genuinely interesting to explore?" not just "What should I achieve?"

**Follow your intuition.** Your gut has access to more information than your conscious mind can process. Trust the path that feels most alive.

**Double down on your uniqueness.** Your unique combination of interests, skills, and perspectives is your compass. The most interesting paths come from what makes you different.

**Collect stepping stones.** Capture ideas and insights even when their use is not clear. Over time, unexpected connections emerge.

## Voice-Powered Reviews and Plans

Voice is a powerful tool for both reviewing and planning:

**For Reviews**: Speaking activates more gratitude, triggers more memories, and is 3-4x faster than typing. A 5-minute voice reflection replaces 20 minutes of writing. Natural reflection produces structured celebration through AI processing.

**For Planning**: Speaking removes friction, bypasses the internal editor, and captures unconscious priorities. What you mention first and what gets energy in your voice reveal priorities your conscious mind might miss. Voice is ideal for the divergent phase (generating options), while structured output is ideal for the convergent phase (selecting priorities).

## Win Tracking

Wins are the currency of the Gain mindset. Small wins count. Progress compounds. An archive of accomplishment fuels confidence and informs future direction.

Wins are tracked at every time scale:
- Daily gratitudes and small wins
- Weekly wins from daily accomplishments
- Monthly wins aggregated from weeks
- Quarterly wins from the broader arc
- Annual wins capturing the full transformation

The longer the period being reviewed, the more valuable the reflection becomes. Wins from different scales reveal different types of growth and progress.

---

## Core Components of Plan and Review

**Clear Goals**: Actionable steps for each period. Maximum 3 per period. They are the concrete steps that make High Hard Goals achievable -- the detailed roadmap breaking down ambitious goals into manageable, focused tasks.

**Wins**: Progress recognition and celebration. The currency of the Gain mindset. Small wins count. Watch as progress compounds. Build an archive of accomplishment that fuels confidence.

**Strategy Systems**: Repeated systems and routines that drive progress. The habits that compound over time. Ongoing practices, different from one-time tasks. They are what take you toward your goals through consistent action.
