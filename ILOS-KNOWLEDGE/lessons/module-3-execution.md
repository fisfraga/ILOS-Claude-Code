# Module 3: Execution

Execution is the Earth element of the Intentional Life Design (ILD) system -- the grounding force that transforms vision and plans into tangible reality. Without execution, vision remains a dream and plans remain ideas. With execution, you build your life one action at a time.

> "Goals without Projects are dreams." -- Tiago Forte
>
> "You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your Systems." -- James Clear

---

## From Plans to Action

Execution is about transforming Clear Goals into daily action and measurable progress. There are two main categories of action:

1. **Projects**: Finite endeavors with clear outcomes (one-time pursuits)
2. **Systems**: Repeated strategic systems of actions (ongoing routines)

Both are essential. Projects give you destinations. Systems give you the vehicle to reach them.

---

## The Components of Execution

### Projects

Projects are finite endeavors with clear outcomes. They are active initiatives with deadlines, organized through the Life Overview, and associated with Areas and High Hard Goals.

**Two essential elements of a project:**
- A clear goal or desired outcome
- A deadline or timeline (even if approximate)

**Projects are distinct from:**
- Dreams (lack specific timelines)
- Hobbies (lack specific outcomes)
- Areas (ongoing, never complete)

**The 10-15 Active Projects Rule:**
- Maximum 15: More than 15 means no visible progress on any
- Minimum 10: You will get stuck on any project eventually; having 10+ means you always have something to switch to
- Use Backlog status for committed but not-yet-active projects

**Project statuses**: Active (currently working on, limit 10-15), Backlog (committed but not started), Completed (achieved the outcome), Abandoned (decided not to pursue).

**Maintenance rhythm:**
- Weekly: Check progress, identify next actions
- Quarterly: Refresh entire list, promote from backlog, break down new mega-projects

### Tasks

Tasks are single, completable actions -- the atoms of execution. They are what you actually DO to make progress. Each task is a concrete step with a clear "done" state.

**Best practices for tasks:**
- Write clear, actionable statements ("Call John about the proposal" not "John")
- Connect to a project, area, or time period
- Break large tasks into smaller steps
- Defer intentionally when not completed -- make deferring a conscious choice, do not let tasks linger

### Notes

Notes are units of captured knowledge: insights and ideas, highlights from books and articles, reflections and learnings, research and reference material.

**Best practices for notes:**
- Capture immediately; do not trust your memory
- Connect to relevant Area, Project, or Topic
- Review and process regularly

### Strategy Systems

Strategy Systems are repeated systems of actions -- the routines that take you toward your goals. They are ongoing practices tied to Areas of life, fundamentally different from one-time tasks or finite projects.

> "You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your Systems." -- James Clear

**What a Strategy System is:**
- A repeated system of actions at a defined periodicity (daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly)
- Connected to a specific Area of life
- A routine that drives consistent progress
- Different from projects (finite) and tasks (one-time)

**Where strategies come from:** Strategies emerge from Areas (the standards you maintain) and Goals (the outcomes you pursue). Areas and Goals define WHAT you want to achieve. Strategy Systems define HOW you will get there.

**Example:**
- Area: Health and Fitness
- Goal: "Reach 15% Body Fat"
- Strategies:
  - Daily: 20 minutes morning movement
  - Weekly: Two 60-minute workout sessions
  - Monthly: Review appointment with nutritionist

**Strategy fields:**
- Periodicity: How often it repeats (daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly)
- Status: Active, Backlog, Completed, Set Aside
- Area: Which life area it serves
- Specific Moment: When in the period it should happen ("Monday morning", "End of month")
- Steps: Specific actions or checklists involved

### Routine Actions

Routine Actions are specific instances of Strategy Systems -- the actual tasks that get done. They connect strategic intentions to daily execution.

When a Strategy says "Weekly financial review," the Routine Action is the specific instance: "Financial review - Week 47."

**How they work:**
1. Strategy surfaces in the dashboard based on its periodicity
2. A Routine Action is created as the specific completable instance
3. You complete, set aside, or defer the action

**The inheritance chain:**
- Area (e.g., Health & Vitality)
  - Strategy (e.g., Morning exercise routine - Daily)
    - Routine Action (e.g., Morning exercise - Today)

Everything connects. When you complete a Routine Action, you are executing a Strategy that serves an Area that has a Vision, Purpose, and place in your Life Overview.

## The Super Folders Concept

The Life Overview (Areas, Projects, Topics) serves as your **retrieval network**. These categories function as Super Folders -- they are collectors. Think of a folder that collects items inside it.

Items such as Notes, Tasks, Meetings, and other objects are the collected items. They are stored into the respective folder categories using fields.

- Create a note and save it to a project using the Project field
- Create a task and connect it to an area using the Area field
- Capture knowledge and connect it to a topic using the Topic field

This creates a system to find everything through your organizational structure:
- Go to any Area --> see all related notes, tasks, and meetings
- Go to any Project --> see everything connected to it
- Go to any Topic --> find all knowledge on that subject

This solves one of the hardest parts of knowledge management: retrieval. You can only apply or transfer knowledge if you retrieve it first.

## How Execution Grounds Plans into Reality

The relationship between the VPER cycle phases is concrete in execution:

**Vision** defines the destination (MTP, High Hard Goals, Intentional Areas).
**Planning** selects the priorities (maximum 3 goals per period).
**Execution** builds the bridge through:
- Projects that advance goals
- Tasks that advance projects
- Strategies that maintain areas
- Routine Actions that execute strategies
- Notes that capture knowledge along the way

**Review** reflects on what was accomplished, feeding insights back into vision and the next planning cycle.

## Principles for Effective Execution

1. **Start with weekly.** The week is the fundamental unit of execution. Focus on weekly planning first, then layer in daily, monthly, quarterly.

2. **Maximum 3 goals per period.** Extreme prioritization creates focus. More than 3 means scattered attention and minimal progress on any.

3. **Active projects: 10-15.** Enough to always have momentum, few enough to see visible progress.

4. **Tasks should be concrete.** Good: "Call John about the proposal." Bad: "John" or "Follow up."

5. **Connect everything.** Tasks to Projects. Notes to Topics. Strategies to Areas. Goals to High Hard Goals. The retrieval network only works if items are connected.

6. **Review before plan.** Always review the previous period before planning the next. This grounds you in the Gain and informs your direction.

7. **Set aside intentionally.** Not completing something is not failure -- it is data. Defer consciously. Do not let tasks linger.

8. **Use AI as a thinking partner.** AI enhances your thinking but you make the final decisions.

9. **Progress over perfection.** The system serves you. Adapt it to your life. Skip a review if needed. Sustainable practice beats perfect adherence.
