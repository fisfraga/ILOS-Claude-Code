# Module 1: Life Vision

Vision is the first pillar of the Intentional Life Design (ILD) system. It is the Fire element -- it illuminates your path and ignites your purpose. Without vision, you might plan productive weeks that lead nowhere meaningful. With vision first, every week serves something bigger, and every goal connects to your purpose.

> "People may spend their whole lives climbing the ladder of success only to find, once they reach the top, that the ladder is leaning against the wrong wall." -- Stephen R. Covey

A Life Vision has two essential parts and one foundational practice:

1. **Life Overview** -- What matters now (the map of your present)
2. **Life Purpose** -- Your why in life (the direction of your future)
3. **Review the Past** -- Your life story (grounding in the Gain before envisioning)

---

## Life Overview: Dimensions of Life

The Life Overview is an externalization of everything in your mind -- roles, responsibilities, deadlines, life situations. It serves as a **retrieval network** for organizing and finding all your knowledge, notes, meetings, contacts, tasks, and more.

Of the four processes in knowledge management (Creation, Storage/Retrieval, Transfer, Application), retrieval is the hardest and most critical. You can only apply or transfer knowledge if you retrieve it first. The Life Overview solves this by creating organizational categories that everything connects to.

### The Three Core Dimensions

**Areas** -- Ongoing responsibilities and life domains that never truly complete. They represent territories you continuously maintain: Health & Fitness, Relationships, Career, Finances, Creative Projects, Spiritual Practice.

Each Area can be made intentional by defining:
- **Vision**: What should this area look like? What is the ideal?
- **Purpose**: Why does this area matter? Why maintain it?
- **Strategy**: What routines and systems keep this area healthy?

**Projects** -- Finite endeavors with clear outcomes and endpoints. A project must have two essential elements: a clear goal or desired outcome, and a deadline or timeline. Projects are distinct from dreams (which lack timelines), hobbies (which lack outcomes), and Areas (which are ongoing).

The 10-15 Active Projects Rule: More than 15 means no visible progress on any. Fewer than 10 means you have nothing to switch to when stuck. Use status tracking: Active, Backlog, Completed, Abandoned.

**Topics** -- Subjects, themes, and areas of interest that capture your curiosity but are not tied to specific outcomes or timelines. Topics are containers for knowledge you want to collect, connect, and return to. Similar to "Resources" in the PARA method.

These three dimensions together form your **Super Folders** -- the categories that collect and organize all items (notes, tasks, meetings) in the system.

## Review the Past: Grounding in the Gain

Before envisioning your future, you must ground yourself in how far you have already come. This is the **Gap to Gain** principle in action.

- **The Gap**: Comparing yourself to an ideal scenario you have not reached yet. Exhausting. Never enough.
- **The Gain**: Measuring against your past self, where you started. Energizing. Always progressing.

When you start from the Gain, you approach your future with gratitude, confidence, and momentum -- not desperation or lack.

### Why Review Past Wins First

- Builds evidence of your capability -- you have already accomplished more than you realize
- Creates foundation for confident future visioning -- past wins fuel belief in what is possible
- Shifts perspective from lack to abundance -- you enter planning from strength, not scarcity
- Connects the dots of your journey -- patterns in your past reveal directions for your future

### Time Horizons to Review

Different time scales reveal different types of growth:
- **Last 10 years**: Major life transformations, identity shifts, big leaps
- **Last 3 years**: Significant achievements, relationships built, skills developed
- **Last 1 year**: Recent accomplishments, growth areas, challenges overcome
- **Last 3 months**: Current momentum, recent wins, emerging patterns

> "You can't connect the dots looking forward; you can only connect them looking backwards." -- Steve Jobs

## Life Purpose: From Curiosity to Action

Life Purpose flows from curiosity through a clear cascade:

**Life Overview --> Favorite Problems --> MTP --> High Hard Goals --> Clear Goals --> Daily Actions**

Everything connects. Every daily action can serve your life purpose.

### Step 1: Favorite Problems -- Find Your Curiosity

Inspired by physicist Richard Feynman's approach to curiosity-driven work: keep a dozen of your favorite problems constantly present in your mind.

> "Every time you hear or read a new trick or a new result, test it against each of your twelve problems to see whether it helps." -- Richard Feynman

Favorite Problems are not tasks to complete but directions to explore. They are questions that fascinate you endlessly, curiosities that follow you across years. Researcher Steven Kotler identifies curiosity as the first strong indicator for finding passion, which fuels purpose.

**Benefits of Favorite Problems:**
- Filter for decisions: identify which information serves your long-term direction
- Focus your learning: concentrate where it makes the highest difference
- Identify MTPs: clusters of problems reveal overarching purposes
- Stay curious: a living reminder of what drives you

**Example structures**: "How can I...?", "How might we...?", "What would be possible if...?", "What would have to be true to...?"

### Step 2: Massively Transformative Purpose (MTP)

An MTP is a purpose that could guide your life's work. Inspired by Flow research and the Exponential Organizations framework.

- **Massively**: Big enough to inspire you for years or decades
- **Transformative**: Changes something meaningful in the world or yourself
- **Purpose**: Connects to a deeper "why" beyond personal benefit

**Traits of a good MTP**: Huge and inspiring. Provides collective impact. Clearly focused. Unique to you. Aimed at radical transformation. Forward-looking.

**What an MTP is NOT**: Something benefiting only yourself. Technology-specific. Representative of what is possible today (it should stretch beyond). Motivated only by profits. Just a big goal without transformative purpose.

**Finding your MTP**: Look for intersections among your Favorite Problems. Where 2-5 curiosities overlap, a purpose often emerges that encompasses multiple things you care deeply about.

You can have **multiple MTPs**. You are not born with one pre-defined purpose. You can change purposes as you grow and cultivate multiple purposes at once.

### Step 3: High Hard Goals (HHGs)

High Hard Goals are major life objectives that take you in the direction of your MTP. They may take years but will completely change your life when achieved. This concept comes from Steven Kotler's Flow research -- goals that bring huge excitement and move you closer to major transformation.

**HHGs serve as alignment tools:**
- Connect UP to your MTP (overarching purpose)
- Connect DOWN to Annual, Quarterly, Monthly, Weekly goals
- They are the stepping stones between purpose and action

**The Goal Cascade:**
MTP --> High Hard Goals --> Annual Goals --> Quarterly Goals --> Monthly Goals --> Weekly Goals --> Daily Actions

**Benefits of HHGs:**
- Heightened attention: you notice opportunities related to your goals
- Amplified persistence: you push through obstacles because the goal matters
- Increased motivation: higher productivity because the work is meaningful

## Intentional Areas: The VPS Framework

For each important Area, you can make it truly intentional by defining its Vision, Purpose, and Strategy. This transforms you from someone who reacts to life into someone who creates their life.

- **Vision** (What you want): Describe the ideal state. What does it look like to live an intentional, thriving life in this area?
- **Purpose** (Why it matters): Articulate the compelling reasons. What energizes you? What empowers you to take action?
- **Strategy** (How you will get there): Outline specific actions, habits, and systems that will make your vision real. Strategies can operate at different periodicities -- daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly.

**Example for Health & Vitality:**
- Vision: "I feel energized and strong. I move daily with joy. I sleep deeply. I eat foods that nourish me."
- Purpose: "My health is the foundation of everything. Without vitality, I cannot show up fully for my loved ones, my work, or my creative expression."
- Strategy: Move body 30-60 minutes daily. Consistent sleep routine. Morning breathwork. Meal prep twice weekly. Quarterly health check-ups.

## The Relationship Between Overview and Purpose

The Life Overview and Life Purpose are complementary lenses:

- **Life Overview** is the map of your present -- where you stand now across all dimensions
- **Life Purpose** is the compass pointing to your future -- where you want to go

The Overview organizes and grounds you. The Purpose inspires and propels you. Together they ensure that the life you are building day by day is the life you actually want to live. Every project, every task, every note can trace its lineage back to an Area you care about or a Purpose you are pursuing. This is the foundation of intentional living.
