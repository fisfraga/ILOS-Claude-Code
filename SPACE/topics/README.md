# Topics

Topics are **interest containers** -- subjects and themes that capture your curiosity but are not tied to deadlines or responsibilities. They are the equivalent of "Resources" in the PARA method.

---

## What Topics Are For

Topics serve three purposes:

1. **Collecting** -- Gather notes, highlights, ideas, and references about a subject in one place
2. **Connecting** -- Link related ideas across different areas and projects
3. **Returning** -- Come back to accumulated knowledge when you need it

Unlike projects (which have outcomes and deadlines) or areas (which require ongoing attention), topics are pressure-free containers for your curiosity.

## The Super Folders Concept

Topics, along with Areas and Projects, serve as **Super Folders** -- retrieval gateways in your knowledge system. The core idea:

> "If you can't find your notes, you can't use your notes."

When you capture a note, article highlight, or idea, you tag it with the relevant Topic (and optionally an Area or Project). Later, when you open that Topic, everything you have ever captured about it is there -- organized and retrievable.

This replaces the traditional folder hierarchy with a **network of connections**. A single note can belong to multiple Topics, an Area, and a Project simultaneously -- just like ideas in your mind.

### How Super Folders Work

1. **Tag information** when you capture it (assign to a Topic, Area, or Project)
2. **Retrieve information** when you need it (open the Super Folder, see everything connected)
3. **Cross-reference** naturally (a note about "Flow States" might connect to both the "Psychology" topic and the "Creative Expression" area)

## Maintenance

Topics are low-maintenance by design. You do not need to review them on a schedule. They grow organically as you encounter relevant information. During quarterly reviews, you might notice which topics are actively growing and which have gone dormant -- this can be a signal about where your curiosity is currently pointing.
