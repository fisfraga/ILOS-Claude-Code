# Projects

Projects are **outcomes you are committed to achieving**, with clear deliverables and deadlines. Unlike Areas (which are ongoing), Projects have a finish line. They are the vehicle through which your goals become reality.

---

## What Makes a Good Project

A well-defined project has:
- **A clear outcome** -- What does "done" look like?
- **A deadline** -- When should this be completed? (Even if approximate)
- **A connected area** -- Which of your 12 Key Areas does this serve?
- **A status** -- Is it Active, Backlog, Completed, or Abandoned?
- **(Optional) A connected goal** -- Which HHG, annual, or quarterly goal does this serve?

---

## The 10-15 Active Projects Rule

Maintain **10-15 active projects** at any time:

- **Fewer than 10** = you are stalling. Always have something to switch to when energy shifts.
- **More than 15** = you are scattered. No visible progress on anything.

Refresh your project list weekly (quick scan) and quarterly (deep review).

---

## Project Statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Active** | Currently receiving attention and effort |
| **Backlog** | Defined but not yet started -- waiting for the right time |
| **Completed** | Outcome achieved, deliverable shipped |
| **Abandoned** | Intentionally stopped -- not a failure, a decision |

Abandoning a project is not failure. It is intelligence -- recognizing that your energy is better spent elsewhere.

## How Projects Connect to ILOS

- **Areas** (SPACE) give projects context -- every project serves at least one area
- **Goals** (TIME) give projects priority -- "Ship the course" is a quarterly goal that drives the "Online Course" project
- **Strategy Systems** (TIME) support projects -- "Write 500 words daily" is a strategy that serves the "Book Draft" project
- **Wins** (MEMORY) celebrate project progress -- "Completed first chapter draft" is a win tagged to the project's area

## Projects as Super Folders

In Tana (if configured), each project becomes a **Super Folder** -- a retrieval gateway that collects all related notes, tasks, meetings, and references. Open a project, see everything connected to it. This solves the hardest PKM problem: finding what you captured.

Even without Tana, you can achieve this by keeping project-related files in a project directory and referencing the project name in your wins and goals.
